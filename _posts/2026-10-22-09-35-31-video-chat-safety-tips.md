# Video Chat Safety: Practical Rules for Talking to Random Strangers

Video chat safety advice is usually written for parents of teenagers and mostly says "don't." ## The seven rules that actually reduce risk

1. **Never reveal identifying details early.** Full name, city, workplace, university, daily routes — any one of them plus a face is enough to find someone. "I'm from Europe" is a complete answer for call one.
2. **Keep it on the platform.** The moment a stranger pushes you to another messenger, a dating app, or a "private show" link, the moderation you were under stops applying.
3. **Never send money, never accept it.** The classic random-chat scams are a sob story with a payment link. A stranger you met 40 seconds ago wanting $20 for anything is a red flag, not a coincidence.
4. **Watch for recording.** Assume any stranger could screen-record your side. This is where the platform matters: Aura's video is peer-to-peer and not recorded by the service, and your partner sees you only during the call.
5. **Use the exit tools.** Next and Report exist for a reason. Pressing Next on someone weird is not rude; it's the design working. Hesitation is the actual risk.
6. **Cover your face when you want to.** Aura's optional emoji mask mode means "on video" doesn't have to mean "recognized." Great for testing a platform before committing your face.
7. **Check the age gate and mean it.** Random video chat is 18+ for reasons that go beyond paperwork. Aura enforces 18+ with AI moderation watching the feed.
