**Telegram mini apps** are the quietest platform shift of the last few years: full web applications that launch inside a Telegram chat, with no app store, no install, and no separate login. ## What Telegram mini apps actually are

A Telegram mini app is a web app that opens inside the Telegram interface through the Bot API. You tap a bot or a button, and a full-screen application loads — camera, microphone, payments, the works — without leaving the messenger. Three properties make the category different from "website on your phone":

- **Zero install.** No app store, no download size, no update cycle. The app is a URL that Telegram frames.
- **Zero signup.** Telegram already knows who you are. There is no email, no password, no "verify your phone" wall.
- **Distribution by link.** A mini app spreads as a bot link — like [https://t.me/you_aura_bot](https://t.me/you_aura_bot) — not as an App Store listing competing with a million others.

For a while, mini apps mostly meant tap-games and crypto toys. The format's real strength is smaller, social, impulse-driven software — exactly the stuff app-store friction killed.

## Why random video chat was the perfect first real use case

Random video chat is an impulse product: nobody plans to meet a stranger, and every second of friction between the impulse and the first call loses users. This is the exact graveyard where the category's successors buried themselves after Omegle shut down in November 2023 — clones that demanded a download, an account, a subscription. Chatroulette and OmeTV still live outside your messenger and still push their own apps.

## Why this format wins for social products specifically

Mini apps fail for products that need a persistent identity — nobody replays a game they have to re-login to. Social experiments are the opposite: the value is in the next stranger, not in your account. A messenger-hosted app inherits distribution (a bot link is forwardable), identity (Telegram session), and monetization-free simplicity in one package. Aura is the proof case: no app store review, no install funnel, no onboarding flow, and a product that fits on one permission screen. The old random-chat sites couldn't do this because they predate the format; the new clones can't do it because their business model needs the app icon on your home screen. Aura's bet is that the messenger is the platform now, and the six-tap flow is what that bet looks like from the user's side.
