# Best Omegle Alternatives 2026: An Honest Comparison of Six Random Video Chats

Omegle shut down in November 2023, and the scramble for its throne has been messy. Two years later, the **best Omegle alternatives 2026** are a mix of genuinely decent apps, ad farms with a video window, and one quiet newcomer that took a different route entirely: a random video chat that lives inside Telegram. We compared six options on the things that actually matter after you click Start — sign-up friction, moderation, privacy, and whether you can skip a bad match in one tap.

## What made our shortlist

We only included services where you can reach a live stranger on video within about a minute, on desktop or mobile, without paying upfront. Every one is 18+. Here they are, ranked by how little they get in your way — which is, after all, what people loved about Omegle.

### 1. Aura — random video chat inside Telegram

Aura ([t.me/you_aura_bot](https://t.me/you_aura_bot)) is a Telegram Mini App: no download, no separate registration, no new password to forget. You pick your gender and one of 20+ languages, hit Start, and you're in a live P2P video call with a stranger who speaks your language. If "open an app I already use, press one button, talk to someone" is your whole requirement, nothing else on this list is faster.

### 2. OmeTV — the crowd favorite

OmeTV absorbed much of Omegle's refugee traffic and still has the biggest random-chat audience. Matching is fast in most countries, and the app is free with account options for extra controls. Downsides: it's a separate app with its own registration, the ad load is noticeable, and moderation quality visibly fluctuates by region and hour.

### 3. Chatrandom — the filter veteran

Chatrandom's strength is mature filtering: language, country, and interest filters that actually work. The interface feels a decade old, and several useful features sit behind a subscription. A reasonable pick if you want control more than volume.

### 4. Bazo — good reporting, pushy billing

Bazo built a solid user-reporting pipeline, which matters more than most features on this list. But it requires sign-up before you see anyone, and the payment prompts are aggressive. Decent moderation, tiresome monetization.

### 5. Camsurf — lightweight and strict-ish

Camsurf is browser-friendly, relatively clean, and leans on community flagging plus moderation. The matching pool is thinner outside the US/EU, and free-tier users get time pressure that pushes upgrades.

### 6. Chamet — social features over 1-on-1

Chamet is technically a random video chat, but it drifts toward gifting, virtual gifts, and creator-style dynamics. Fun if you like that economy; frustrating if you just want an equal two-way conversation.

### Are random video chats safe in 2026?

Safer than 2022, not risk-free. Look for: enforced 18+, visible moderation (human or AI), one-tap reporting, and clarity on recording. Aura, for example, runs P2P calls that aren't recorded server-side; others store clips. Never treat "random chat" as a private channel for IDs, addresses, or money.
