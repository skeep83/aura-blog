## The direct successors: what each one is like now

**Chatroulette.** The original is alive, added moderation and account requirements, and never shook the reputation that made it famous. It works, but it's an app-and-website product from an earlier era: global roulette, no language filter, paid features pushed at you.

**OmeTV.** The biggest winner of the shutdown, and the most competent clone. It works, but on mobile it wants its own app installed, it wants an account, and the feed is still a global firehose — you get no filter for language, so conversations die at "hi, from where?" more often than they should.

**The clone sites.** Hundreds of "Omegle alternative" pages running the same script: a grid of strangers, aggressive ads, and moderation you have to take on faith. Most exist to monetize search traffic, not to host conversations.

The common failure: every one of them rebuilt Omegle's layout but none rebuilt the friction profile. Omegle was one page and one button. Its successors are funnels.
